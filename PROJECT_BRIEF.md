# Hello Tagbilaran — Product Brief

## Product statement

Hello Tagbilaran is an immersive tourism and local-discovery website for Tagbilaran City, Bohol. It combines an editorial history experience, an illustrated interactive map, and practical visitor planning tools.

**Working tagline:** Where every street leads to a story.

## Product goals

- Help visitors understand Tagbilaran before asking them to explore it.
- Make history memorable through a scroll-driven paper narrative.
- Help people discover heritage, food, accommodations, shopping, events, outdoor spaces, and essentials.
- Give local businesses and community stories meaningful visibility.
- Prevent geographic confusion between Tagbilaran, nearby municipalities, and wider Bohol.
- Work well on modest phones and inconsistent mobile connections.

## Primary audiences

1. First-time visitors arriving in Bohol.
2. Domestic travelers planning a short city stay.
3. Residents looking for food, events, or overlooked city places.
4. Families, seniors, and accessibility-conscious travelers.
5. Students and researchers exploring local history.

## Core journey

1. The visitor lands on a paper cover with the title “Hello Tagbilaran.”
2. Scrolling down unfolds a chronological story of the city.
3. Historic chapters combine short text, archival-style imagery, captions, dates, and illustrated marginalia.
4. The final chapter says: “The story continues in the streets of Tagbilaran.”
5. A leftward gesture or explicit button folds the history into a city map.
6. The visitor filters places, opens postcard-style place sheets, and saves stops.
7. Saved stops become a printable/shareable paper itinerary and digital city passport.

## History chapters

Treat these as editorial chapters, not as unverified final copy:

1. Early coastal settlement and regional trade.
2. Sandugo and the idea of friendship.
3. Spanish-era civic, religious, and urban development.
4. War, rebuilding, and the road to cityhood.
5. Tagbilaran today: markets, schools, tricycles, cafés, festivals, and waterfront life.

Every historical claim must be cited in the content source notes before publication.

## Signature features

### Then & Now

A draggable comparison between an archival image and the present-day view. Provide an accessible two-image alternative and captions.

### Voices of Tagbilaran

Short resident audio stories with English, Filipino, and Bisaya transcripts. Consent, attribution, and transcript review are required.

### Digital City Passport

Visitors collect illustrated stamps for meaningful activities. Stamps are delightful progress markers, not location surveillance.

### Build My Tagbilaran Day

Generate a suggested route from time, budget, interests, mobility needs, group type, weather, and transportation preference. Initial release may use deterministic local rules rather than AI.

### Festival Mode

An optional seasonal theme for Sandugo and Saulog with verified schedules, routes, notices, and accessibility information.

## Scope boundary

The first milestone is a content-rich front-end prototype. It does not require accounts, booking, payments, live business dashboards, or a production CMS.

## Success criteria

- A visitor understands the concept within the first screen.
- The story-to-map transition is discoverable without instruction.
- A place can be found in three interactions or fewer from the map screen.
- All essential functions work using keyboard and reduced-motion settings.
- The site earns its visual identity through content and craft, not decorative clutter.
